A year ago, a junior engineer asked me if I was worried about AI taking my job. I told them the truth: AI changed *how* I build almost completely, and changed *what I'm responsible for* not at all.

## AI raises everyone's floor

The boilerplate, the scaffolding, the third unit test that's almost identical to the first two — AI does that now, in seconds, better than I'd do it tired at 6pm. The floor of what any engineer can produce in an hour has risen dramatically.

That's genuinely great. The drudgery was never the point.

## Taste is still the ceiling

But here's what didn't change: someone still has to know *what's worth building*. Someone has to recognise when the confident, beautifully-formatted answer is subtly, dangerously wrong. Someone has to own the architecture, the edge cases, and the consequences when it ships.

That someone is still me. AI is the most capable junior teammate I've ever had — fast, tireless, occasionally and confidently incorrect. You don't hand a junior the keys to production without review, and you don't hand them your judgement either.

## My rules

- **I never ship what I can't explain.** If I can't defend a line in code review, it doesn't go in, no matter how clean it looks.
- **Verify, then trust.** AI accelerates the draft. Correctness is mine.
- **Leverage, not replacement.** I use it to remove friction and amplify intent, so a small team can ship like a large one — without lowering the bar.

## Where it actually fits

Code generation for the obvious 80%. First-pass review to catch the easy stuff before a human looks. Architecture conversations where I need a sparring partner. Documentation I'd otherwise never write. Research that compresses a learning curve. And rapid prototyping — exploring three directions in the time it used to take to build one, so the best idea wins instead of the first.

The engineers who'll thrive aren't the ones who resist this, and they're not the ones who outsource their thinking to it. They're the ones who use it as leverage while keeping their hands firmly on the wheel.

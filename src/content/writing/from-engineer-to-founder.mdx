I write software for a living, and I'm increasingly drawn to problems that have nothing to do with screens. That's not a contradiction. It's the most natural progression I can imagine.

## What software taught me

Four years of engineering at enterprise scale taught me a way of thinking that turns out to be domain-agnostic:

- **Systems thinking** — seeing the whole, not just the part in front of me.
- **Iteration** — shipping something small, learning, and improving, instead of waiting for perfect.
- **Relentless attention to the user** — because elegant solutions that help no one are just hobbies.

None of that is specific to JavaScript. It's a builder's operating system, and it runs on any problem.

## Why beyond software

The most interesting problems of the next decade aren't only digital. They're physical, environmental, and stubbornly real: how we make things, what we make them from, and what happens when we're done with them.

I'm drawn to bio-compostable materials and climate technology because they sit exactly where engineering rigour meets genuine impact. A product designed to return to the earth instead of outliving its usefulness in a landfill is, quite simply, a better-designed product.

## The thesis

> The same instinct that drives me to remove friction from an interface drives me to remove waste from a supply chain.

I don't want to abandon engineering for entrepreneurship. I want to fuse them. Software and automation can make small-scale, sustainable manufacturing economically competitive with the wasteful conventional alternative. That's a software problem wearing a hardware costume — and it's exactly the kind of thing my background prepares me for.

## Where I am honestly

Early. I'm researching materials science, studying the economics, running bench experiments, and learning in public. I don't have a company yet. What I have is a direction and a builder's conviction that the gap between "good intentions" and "viable business" is closed by exactly the discipline software taught me.

The plan: Frontend Engineer → Product Engineer → Founder. Not as an escape from engineering, but as its logical extension.

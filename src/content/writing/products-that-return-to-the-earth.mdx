In software, we obsess over the full lifecycle of a system — how it's built, how it scales, how it's maintained, how it's eventually deprecated. We'd never ship a service with no plan for what happens at end of life. Yet that's exactly how most physical products are made.

## The end is a design constraint

The book *Cradle to Cradle* reframed something for me: waste is a design failure, not an inevitability. If a product becomes garbage the moment it's used, that outcome was designed in — by omission — at the very start.

What if end-of-life were a first-class design constraint? What if "this returns safely to the earth" sat in the spec right next to "this is cheap to produce" and "this feels good to use"?

That's the question behind bio-compostable products. Not "how do we dispose of this less badly," but "how do we design something whose end is as considered as its beginning."

## Builders are well-positioned for this

The instinct that makes a good engineer — caring about the whole system, not just the happy path — is exactly the instinct sustainability needs. We already think in lifecycles. We already treat "what happens when this fails or ends" as part of the design. Applying that to materials and manufacturing is less of a leap than it looks.

## The hard part is economics

Compostable alternatives exist. The reason they aren't everywhere is cost and performance: they have to be invisible to the user and competitive on price, or they stay a niche premium.

That's where it gets interesting for someone with a software background. Automation, process optimisation, and smart small-scale manufacturing are levers that can close the cost gap. The challenge isn't only chemistry — it's systems and economics, which is familiar territory.

## A better-designed product

Strip away the virtue signalling and the case is simple: a product that does its job *and* returns to the earth is just better designed than one that does its job and lingers in a landfill for centuries. Better design is something builders can get genuinely excited about.

That's the thread I'm pulling on next.
